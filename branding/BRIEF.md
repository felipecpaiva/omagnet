# omagnet brand brief

The brief behind every logo variation. Written 2026-11-05, before any image was made, so the variations can be judged
against it and not against taste alone.

## What the project is, in one line

Magnet-style window snapping for Omarchy's floating workspaces: a shortcut or a drag to a screen edge, and a window
snaps to a half, third, quarter or the whole screen.

## Who it is for

Omarchy and Hyprland users who want their windows to snap the way they did on a Mac. Terminal-comfortable people who like
small, sharp tools, who read a README before they install, and who will judge the project in the few seconds it takes to see
its logo and its first screenshot.

## What the brand has to say

1. **Snap.** Things click into place, exactly. Order from clutter.
2. **At home on Omarchy.** It looks like it belongs beside Omarchy's own apps and themes, without pretending to be official.
3. **Small and precise.** Not a big platform. A sharp utility.

## Name

`omagnet`, written lowercase, one word. The `o` is Omarchy's habit (omarchy, the community's `oma...` plugins) and
`magnet` is the Mac app it follows. Say it "oh-magnet".

## References, and what not to take from them

| Reference | What to learn from it | What must NOT be copied |
|-----------|----------------------|-------------------------|
| **Magnet** (macOS, by BOOTCODE A.S., icon read from its public App Store listing) | Its idea: windows as tiles that fill a screen. Its tone: friendly, plain, a rounded app icon. | Its icon: the blue rounded square, the round inset, and the exact tile layout (one tall tile on the left, two stacked on the right). Its name styling. Do not imply a link to it. |
| **Omarchy** (the logo and icon in `/usr/share/omarchy`) | Its look: chunky pixel blocks with notched corners, a half-block terminal wordmark, a single bright green on a plain ground, a love of monospace. | Its logo or its icon, or anything that looks like an official Omarchy mark. Omarchy has not endorsed this project. |

Both names appear only as plain text, in the README line "Magnet-style snapping for Omarchy", with the disclaimer in
[README.md](../README.md): not affiliated with or endorsed by either.

## Constraints every variation must meet

- **Works small.** The icon is still readable at 16 pixels and at 32 pixels, in one colour.
- **Works on light and dark.** A one-colour version for each, so it reads on Omarchy's light themes (such as "white")
  and dark ones (such as "tokyo-night").
- **No gradients needed.** The mark must stand without a gradient, shadow or glow. (A gradient may be added as polish.)
- **Vector first.** A real SVG source for the icon and the wordmark; PNG exports come from it.
- **Own idea.** Different from Magnet's and Omarchy's marks at a glance, even in a thumbnail.
- **Text rules.** No em-dash or en-dash anywhere in text; plain English.
- **Licence.** The artwork is ours (made for this project, by an image model and a person, no copied marks) and is released
  with the project under the same MIT licence, unless the maintainer chooses a different licence for the brand.

## Deliverables for the chosen variation

1. Icon: `icon.svg`, PNG at 1024, 512, 256, 128, 64, 32, 16; one-colour light and dark versions; a monochrome mask.
2. Wordmark and horizontal lockup (icon plus name), on light, dark and transparent.
3. GitHub social preview, 1280 by 640 pixels, solid background, under 1 MB.
4. README banner, wide, with the tagline.
5. Marketplace `preview.png` at the plugin repo root (Omarchy's marketplace builds its card images from it).
6. A terminal banner in plain text (half-block letters), for the README and the install output.
7. Social cards for posts (square and wide), a short demo clip or GIF of the snapping, and screenshots of the settings window.
8. A one-page brand sheet: palette with contrast ratios, type, spacing, do and do not.

## How the variations are judged

On a board with each variation's icon at 512, 64 and 16 pixels, on a light and a dark background, next to the Omarchy
and Magnet marks (to check it is not mistaken for either), and its wordmark in a README header. The maintainer picks one;
then the full kit is made for that one only.
